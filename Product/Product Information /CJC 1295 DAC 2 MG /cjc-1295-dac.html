What Is CJC-1295 with DAC?

A long-acting GHRH analogue engineered for sustained pituitary stimulation and extended GH/IGF-1 research.

Natural GHRH is a 44-amino acid hypothalamic hormone that tells the pituitary to release growth hormone. It's released in pulses, cleared from the bloodstream within minutes, and is practically useless as a research tool in its unmodified form. CJC-1295 with DAC is a synthetic solution to this problem: a 29-residue GHRH analogue with stability modifications and an albumin-binding Drug Affinity Complex that keeps the molecule active in circulation for 6 to 8 days from a single dose.

The DAC component — maleimidopropionic acid attached via a lysine linker — reacts with free thiol groups on serum albumin to form a stable, reversible bond. The peptide-albumin complex then circulates slowly, continuously releasing free active peptide that engages GHRH receptors on pituitary somatotroph cells. The result is sustained GH secretion, progressive IGF-1 elevation, and a single-dose pharmacokinetic window spanning a week — conditions impossible to replicate with shorter-acting GHRH peptides.

In laboratory research, CJC-1295 with DAC is used to study: sustained GH axis activation and receptor regulation; chronic IGF-1 elevation and downstream anabolic signalling; body composition changes in animal models over weeks of treatment; and the metabolic consequences of maintained rather than pulsatile GH elevation. It can also be compared directly with CJC-1295 without DAC to isolate the effects of pulsatile versus continuous GH stimulation patterns.

Understanding the Drug Affinity Complex

How a chemical modification extends a peptide's working life from minutes to days.

The Half-Life Problem with GHRH Peptides

Standard GHRH and even optimised GHRH analogues are degraded rapidly by dipeptidyl peptidase IV (DPP-IV), which cleaves at the Ala-Asp bond near the N-terminus, and by other serum proteases. Modified GRF(1-29) — the same 29-residue backbone as CJC-1295 — has a half-life of 30–60 minutes, which is workable for studying acute GH pulses but impractical for research requiring days of sustained GH elevation. Multiple daily injections are required, introducing variability, stress effects, and practical limitations for longer experimental designs.

Mechanism of Action

From GHRH receptor binding to sustained GH release and downstream IGF-1 signalling.

GHRH Receptor Activation

CJC-1295 binds the GHRH receptor (GHRH-R), a Gs-protein-coupled receptor on somatotroph cells of the anterior pituitary. Receptor activation drives adenylate cyclase to produce cyclic AMP (cAMP). Elevated cAMP activates protein kinase A (PKA), which drives two parallel outcomes simultaneously: stimulation of GH gene transcription via CREB phosphorylation (building the GH biosynthesis capacity of somatotrophs over time), and triggering of GH granule exocytosis for immediate release into the portal blood supply. With the DAC-maintained sustained free peptide levels, both of these processes receive continuous stimulation over the 6–8 day pharmacokinetic window.

Sustained vs. Pulsatile GH Patterns

Under natural physiology, GHRH is released in pulses from the hypothalamus while somatostatin provides the inhibitory counterpart — the interplay between them creates the pulsatile GH release pattern with characteristic sleep-associated peaks. CJC-1295 with DAC's continuous GHRH-R stimulation raises the amplitude of each existing GH pulse over time rather than adding new ones. Ionescu and Frohman (2006) demonstrated that pulsatile GH secretion is preserved even during continuous CJC-1295 stimulation — the pulses just occur at a higher amplitude baseline. This distinction matters for researchers studying the physiological consequences of different GH release patterns.

IGF-1 Production and Downstream Effects

Elevated GH from sustained GHRH-R stimulation drives hepatic insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) production. IGF-1 activates its receptor (IGF-1R) in muscle, bone, and adipose tissue, engaging the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway to promote protein synthesis and cell survival. GH also directly activates hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL) in adipocytes, promoting lipolysis. The sustained nature of the GH signal from CJC-1295 with DAC means IGF-1 rises cumulatively over 2–4 days and can remain elevated for 1–2 weeks in some animal models — creating a prolonged window for studying downstream anabolic, metabolic, and body composition effects that simply cannot be achieved with shorter-acting GH secretagogues.

Research Applications

CJC-1295 with DAC's extended half-life opens research questions that shorter-acting GHRH analogues cannot address.

1. Sustained GH Axis Research

The core application is studying what happens to the GH axis under continuous GHRH-R stimulation. Key experimental questions include: how somatotroph GH gene expression adapts to persistent cAMP elevation; whether GHRH-R downregulation occurs and at what timescale; how somatostatin negative feedback adjusts to ongoing stimulation; and what the complete GH and IGF-1 response curve looks like from administration through return to baseline. Ionescu and Frohman (2006) confirmed that pulsatile GH release is maintained even under continuous CJC-1295 stimulation — just at elevated amplitude — which has implications for interpreting pulsatile versus sustained GH effects in other research contexts.

2. Body Composition and Metabolic Research

In preclinical animal models, sustained GH elevation drives measurable body composition shifts over weeks: reduced fat mass (via direct lipolytic effects) and increased lean mass (via IGF-1-mediated protein anabolic signalling). Laboratory protocols track these changes using DEXA, MRI, or direct tissue weighing, alongside serial biochemical measurements of GH, IGF-1, insulin, glucose, and lipid parameters. The chronic glucose metabolism effects are particularly important — sustained GH excess exerts insulin-antagonistic effects that are relevant to research on GH-related metabolic disorders and the safety profile of prolonged GH axis activation.

3. Anabolic Pathway Studies

The sustained IGF-1 elevation from CJC-1295 with DAC treatment allows investigation of cumulative anabolic signalling over days to weeks. Molecular endpoints include mTOR pathway phosphorylation (S6K1, 4E-BP1), myofibrillar protein synthesis rate measurements, muscle fibre cross-sectional area changes, collagen synthesis in connective tissue, and bone formation markers (osteocalcin, bone-specific alkaline phosphatase). These anabolic adaptations require extended GH/IGF-1 elevation to produce measurable changes in tissue architecture — a requirement that the DAC modification uniquely satisfies among GHRH analogues.

4. Reproductive and Growth Model Research

GHRH analogues have been investigated for roles in reproductive physiology, with research from the early 1990s showing that GH and IGF-1 levels rise during ovulation and that GH secretagogues can promote superovulation in animal models. CJC-1295's sustained GH stimulation provides a practical tool for studying GH/IGF-1 contributions to reproductive function over multi-day experimental windows. Work in GHRH knockout mice demonstrated that once-daily CJC-1295 administration normalises growth trajectories in animals with impaired endogenous GHRH physiology — confirming the receptor pathway's role in GH-dependent growth and opening models for studying GH replacement strategies.

Storage & Reconstitution

  • Store at –20°C to –80°C in the original sealed vial
  • Protect from light and moisture; desiccated storage environment recommended
  • Stable for 24+ months at –20°C when correctly stored
  • Do not temperature-cycle sealed vials unnecessarily

Reconstitution

  • Use sterile water, bacteriostatic water (0.9% benzyl alcohol), or PBS
  • Add solvent slowly down the inner vial wall; swirl gently for 3–5 minutes
  • Do not shake or vortex; allow complete dissolution before use
  • Target final pH 6.0–7.0 for optimal stability of the DAC-modified peptide
  • Solution should appear clear and colourless when correctly reconstituted

After Reconstitution

  • Store at 4°C for up to 7–10 days
  • Freeze at –20°C in single-use aliquots for longer storage
  • Maximum 2–3 freeze-thaw cycles; prepare aliquots before first freeze
  • Follow standard PPE protocols (lab coat, gloves, safety glasses) throughout

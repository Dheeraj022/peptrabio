## What Is CJC-1295 with DAC?

CJC-1295 with DAC is a synthetic growth hormone releasing hormone (GHRH) analogue built from the first 29 amino acids of natural GHRH, with several amino acid substitutions for enhanced stability and receptor affinity, plus a chemical attachment called the Drug Affinity Complex (DAC) that fundamentally changes how long the peptide stays active in circulation.

Natural GHRH is released from the hypothalamus in brief pulses that last only a few minutes before peptidases in plasma degrade it. Even optimised GHRH analogues like Modified GRF(1-29) — which shares the same 29-residue sequence — have a plasma half-life of only 30 to 60 minutes. CJC-1295 with DAC extends this to approximately 6 to 8 days through a clever piece of chemistry: the DAC component contains a maleimide group that reacts with free cysteine residues on serum albumin, the most abundant protein in blood, to form a stable thioether bond. The peptide then travels with albumin — effectively using the protein as a slow-release depot. As the albumin-peptide complex equilibrates, free active peptide is continuously released back into circulation over days, maintaining GHRH receptor stimulation throughout.

The practical consequence for research is that a single administration of CJC-1295 with DAC can produce sustained elevation of GH and downstream IGF-1 for a week or more in animal models, compared to the multiple daily doses required with the no-DAC version. This makes it a distinctive tool for studying what happens when the GH axis is held in a chronically elevated state rather than driven by the intermittent peaks characteristic of normal pulsatile GHRH physiology.

The DAC modification was originally developed by ConjuChem Biotechnologies, which initially called the DAC-containing molecule simply "CJC-1295." When the peptide began to be supplied without DAC as well, the naming was clarified: the albumin-binding version became CJC-1295 DAC, and the shorter-acting version became CJC-1295 no DAC (or Modified GRF 1-29).

## The DAC Modification Explained

### Why GHRH Peptides Need Stabilisation

Native GHRH is a 44-amino acid peptide that is cleared from plasma within minutes. The primary degradation route is cleavage by dipeptidyl peptidase IV (DPP-IV) at the Ala²-Asp³ bond near the N-terminus. Modified GRF(1-29) addresses this by substituting D-Ala at position 2 and other protective changes, extending the half-life to 30–60 minutes. That's useful for acute pulse studies but impractical for research requiring days-long GH elevation.

### How the DAC Attachment Works

The DAC modification adds maleimidopropionic acid to a lysine residue at the C-terminus of the peptide. The maleimide group is highly reactive with free thiol groups — specifically, the free cysteine residues found on albumin molecules in plasma. When CJC-1295 DAC enters the bloodstream, the maleimide reacts rapidly with albumin to form a thioether bond. The resulting peptide-albumin conjugate circulates with albumin's long plasma half-life (≈19 days in humans) and is protected from renal filtration and enzymatic attack. As the complex slowly dissociates, free CJC-1295 is released to engage GHRH receptors — creating a sustained low-level continuous release profile over 6–8 days.

### Pulsatile vs. Sustained GH — Why the Distinction Matters

Natural GH release is pulsatile, with the largest pulses typically occurring during deep sleep (slow-wave sleep, stages 3 and 4). This pulsatile pattern has functional consequences — it affects GH receptor downregulation, downstream IGF-1 production kinetics, and the sensitivity of target tissues to GH over time. CJC-1295 without DAC generally preserves pulsatile GH release patterns by providing a burst of GHRH receptor stimulation followed by clearance, allowing natural rhythms to continue. CJC-1295 with DAC replaces this with continuous receptor stimulation, raising the baseline amplitude of existing GH pulses over many days. Researchers comparing the biological outcomes of these two patterns can use the two variants as experimental tools for this question.

## Mechanism of Action

### GHRH Receptor Signalling

The GHRH receptor (GHRH-R) is a Gs-protein-coupled receptor expressed primarily on somatotroph cells of the anterior pituitary. When CJC-1295 binds GHRH-R, the Gs protein activates adenylate cyclase, raising intracellular cyclic AMP (cAMP). Elevated cAMP activates protein kinase A (PKA), which drives two parallel outcomes: transcription and synthesis of new GH protein within somatotroph cells, and exocytosis of existing GH granules into the portal circulation. PKA also phosphorylates CREB, activating GH gene transcription for sustained production rather than just immediate release.

### Sustained GH Release Dynamics

With the DAC modification maintaining free peptide levels continuously, GHRH-R on somatotrophs receives ongoing stimulation rather than periodic pulses. In preclinical models, GH levels begin rising within hours of administration, peak at 6–48 hours depending on the animal model and dose, and remain elevated for multiple days. This extended profile produces cumulative IGF-1 elevation that peaks 2–4 days post-administration and can remain above baseline for 1–2 weeks — a timeframe impractical to achieve with shorter-acting GHRH analogues.

### Downstream IGF-1 and Anabolic Signalling

GH released from the pituitary binds GH receptors in the liver and peripheral tissues, driving hepatic IGF-1 synthesis. IGF-1 circulates bound to binding proteins (IGFBPs) and activates the IGF-1 receptor (IGF-1R) in muscle, bone, adipose tissue, and other targets. IGF-1R activation engages the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway, promoting protein synthesis, cell proliferation, and anti-apoptotic signalling. GH also has direct receptor-mediated effects in adipose tissue through hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL) activation, promoting lipolysis independently of IGF-1. The combination of direct GH effects and IGF-1-mediated anabolic signalling over extended periods is what makes CJC-1295 with DAC useful for chronic body composition and metabolic research.

## Research Applications

### 1. Sustained GH Axis Investigation

CJC-1295 with DAC is the primary tool for studying what happens to the GH axis under conditions of chronic, uninterrupted GHRH receptor stimulation. Key research questions include: how somatotroph GH gene expression responds to persistent cAMP elevation; whether and when GHRH receptor downregulation occurs; how somatostatin negative feedback adapts to continuous stimulation; and what the long-term IGF-1 elevation curve looks like across different animal models. The 6–8 day half-life allows researchers to design studies with a single administration followed by observations across the full pharmacokinetic window.

### 2. Metabolic Research

Sustained GH elevation via CJC-1295 with DAC produces measurable shifts in metabolic parameters over weeks of treatment in preclinical models. Lipolysis in adipose tissue is enhanced as GH sensitises fat cells to lipase activity. Nitrogen balance studies consistently show positive nitrogen retention. Glucose metabolism research is particularly important here, because chronic GH excess produces insulin-antagonistic effects in the liver and skeletal muscle — a relevant safety consideration to study. Laboratory protocols measure serial insulin sensitivity assessments, glucose tolerance tests, and lipid profiles alongside body composition endpoints.

### 3. Body Composition and Anabolic Pathway Research

Extended CJC-1295 with DAC treatment in animal models produces body composition changes visible on DEXA and by direct tissue weighing: fat mass reduction alongside lean mass accretion. These changes are mediated through the direct lipolytic effects of GH and the protein-anabolic effects of elevated IGF-1. Molecular studies track mTOR pathway activation, myofibrillar protein synthesis rates, muscle fibre hypertrophy, and connective tissue collagen production. Because these anabolic changes require weeks to produce measurable differences in tissue mass, the sustained GH elevation from DAC-modified peptide is practically necessary.

### 4. Reproductive Physiology Research

Research from the early 1990s identified that GH and IGF-1 levels rise during ovulation in multiple species and that exogenous GH secretagogues can promote superovulation in animal models. CJC-1295 provides a more sustained GH-stimulating intervention than acute GH or short-acting GHRH analogues, making it useful for studying GH/IGF-1's contributions to reproductive function over multi-day experimental windows. There is also ongoing interest in GH secretagogues in models of impaired fertility, though this is a less developed research area.

### 5. Growth and Development Models

In GHRH knockout mice — animals that lack functional endogenous GHRH — CJC-1295 administration has been shown to restore normal GH secretion and normalise growth trajectories with once-daily dosing. This makes it valuable for studying GH-dependent developmental biology and for modelling GHRH deficiency states in which the consequences of restored versus absent GH signalling can be compared systematically.

## Storage and Reconstitution

### Lyophilized Powder

Store sealed vials at –20°C to –80°C. Protect from light and moisture; desiccated storage is recommended. Stable for 24+ months at –20°C when correctly stored. Avoid temperature cycling of sealed vials.

### Reconstitution

Use sterile water, bacteriostatic water (0.9% benzyl alcohol), or PBS. Add solvent slowly down the inner wall of the vial; swirl gently for 3–5 minutes until fully dissolved. Do not shake or vortex. Target pH 6.0–7.0. Solution should be clear and colourless when ready.

### After Reconstitution

Store at 4°C for up to 7–10 days. For longer storage, freeze at –20°C in single-use aliquots. Limit freeze-thaw cycles to 2–3 maximum. Follow standard PPE protocols throughout reconstitution and handling.

# CJC-1295 (No DAC) – Mod GRF 1-29 (Research Grade)

## Product overview

CJC-1295 (No DAC), also known as Modified GRF 1‑29 or Mod GRF (1‑29), is a 29‑amino acid peptide analog based on the biologically active N‑terminal region of growth hormone‑releasing hormone (GHRH).[web:143][web:317][web:324] It incorporates four strategic amino acid substitutions that improve metabolic stability, increase receptor affinity, and protect the peptide from rapid degradation by enzymes such as DPP‑IV in research models.[web:143][web:322][web:324]

This product is supplied as a high‑purity lyophilised powder for in‑vitro experiments and approved preclinical animal studies only. It is not a drug, supplement, or cosmetic and is not approved for any form of human or veterinary administration.[web:317][web:321][web:323]

## Identity and peptide sequence

CJC‑1295 (No DAC) reproduces the first 29 amino acids of human GHRH and includes C‑terminal amidation for improved stability.[web:143][web:319][web:324] It is often described as the “short‑acting” component of the CJC‑1295 platform because it does not include the Drug Affinity Complex (DAC) that extends half‑life via albumin binding.[web:152][web:322][web:324]

### Sequence

Tyr‑D‑Ala‑Asp‑Ala‑Ile‑Phe‑Thr‑Gln‑Ser‑Tyr‑Arg‑Lys‑Val‑Leu‑Ala‑Gln‑Leu‑Ser‑Ala‑Arg‑Lys‑Leu‑Leu‑Gln‑Asp‑Ile‑Leu‑Ser‑Arg‑NH₂[web:317][web:323]

### Key substitutions (stability-focused)

- D‑Ala at position 2 – helps resist cleavage by DPP‑IV. [web:143][web:322][web:324]  
- Gln at position 8 – reduces deamidation and improves chemical stability. [web:143][web:322]  
- Ala at position 15 – enhances bioactivity in receptor assays. [web:143][web:322]  
- Leu at position 27 – helps prevent oxidation compared with the native residue. [web:143][web:322]

These changes preserve GHRH‑like receptor activation while extending functional life in research settings.[web:143][web:324]

## Scientific background (non‑clinical)

### Mechanism of action in research

In preclinical models, CJC‑1295 (No DAC) acts as a selective agonist at the GHRH receptor on pituitary somatotroph cells.[web:143][web:319][web:324] Receptor activation stimulates Gs‑protein signalling, increases intracellular cAMP, and activates protein kinase A, culminating in pulsatile release of endogenous growth hormone.[web:143][web:322][web:324]

Because it lacks the DAC moiety, this peptide produces brief GH pulses with a plasma half‑life of roughly 30 minutes, closer to natural hypothalamic GHRH signalling than long‑acting DAC analogs.[web:152][web:322][web:324]

### Comparison with DAC variants

Both CJC‑1295 with DAC and CJC‑1295 (No DAC) are built on the same GHRH (1‑29) core, but the DAC‑modified form binds to albumin and shows a markedly prolonged half‑life measured in days rather than minutes.[web:152][web:322] The No‑DAC version is therefore preferred in experiments where researchers want tight control over GH pulse timing and recovery between pulses.[web:143][web:324]

## Representative research applications

Researchers use CJC‑1295 (No DAC) as a tool compound in endocrine and metabolic research. Common application themes include:

- **Pulsatile GH secretion studies** – modelling physiological GH pulses and examining somatotroph responsiveness to short GHRH‑like stimuli.[web:143][web:319][web:324]  
- **GH/IGF‑1 axis investigation** – assessing how repeated pulses influence IGF‑1 production and feedback regulation in preclinical models.[web:319][web:323][web:324]  
- **Combination studies with GHRPs** – exploring synergistic GH release when Mod GRF 1‑29 is paired with ghrelin‑mimetic peptides such as GHRP‑2, GHRP‑6, or ipamorelin.[web:143][web:322][web:324]  
- **Metabolic and body‑composition research** – investigating downstream effects on protein synthesis, lipolysis, and energy balance in animal and cell‑based systems.[web:319][web:323]

All uses remain restricted to controlled in‑vitro assays and approved preclinical experiments.

## Handling, reconstitution, and storage

### Lyophilised powder

- Store unopened vials at −20 °C or below in a dry, dark environment.  
- Protect from moisture and avoid unnecessary temperature cycling.[web:319][web:321][web:323]

### Reconstitution

- Reconstitute with sterile water, bacteriostatic water, or an appropriate buffer using aseptic technique.[web:319][web:321]  
- Add solvent slowly along the vial wall and swirl gently until completely dissolved; avoid vigorous shaking.

### Reconstituted solutions

- Keep at 2–8 °C for short‑term use, protected from light. [web:319][web:321]  
- For longer storage, aliquot and freeze at −20 °C or below to minimise repeated freeze–thaw cycles. [web:319][web:321]  
- Discard any solution that becomes cloudy, discoloured, or shows particulate matter.

Each laboratory should validate stability conditions for its own assays and follow internal SOPs.

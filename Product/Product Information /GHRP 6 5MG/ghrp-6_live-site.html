Scientific overview

GHRP-6 is a synthetic growth hormone secretagogue that binds the ghrelin receptor GHSR-1a and is widely used to study growth hormone release in pituitary and hypothalamic models.[web:275][web:277][web:278]

Because it also produces appetite-related and gastrointestinal effects, the peptide is useful for broader ghrelin-system research rather than GH signalling alone.[web:274][web:278][web:284]

Mechanistic themes in research

GHSR-1a activation

In research models, GHRP-6 activates GHSR-1a with downstream signalling that includes phospholipase C, calcium mobilisation, and protein kinase C-linked responses.[web:277][web:278]

GH pulse studies

The peptide is commonly used to measure acute GH release and to compare single-agent activity with GHRH/GHRP combination protocols.[web:274][web:277][web:278]

Key research application areas

1. GH release and pituitary signalling

Used to examine somatotroph responsiveness, receptor signalling, and short-term GH pulse dynamics in cell and animal models.[web:277][web:278]

2. Appetite and energy balance

Useful in studies of feeding behaviour, hunger signalling, and metabolic regulation because GHRP-6 can produce ghrelin-like appetite effects.[web:275][web:278][web:279]

3. Neuroprotection and tissue repair

Preclinical literature explores memory, wound repair, anti-fibrotic pathways, and cardiac cytoprotection, all in controlled research settings.[web:271][web:272][web:273]

Handling, reconstitution, and storage

Unopened vials

  • Store at −20 °C or below in a dry, dark environment.[web:277][web:278]
  • Keep moisture out and seal tightly after opening.

Reconstitution

  • Reconstitute with sterile water, bacteriostatic water, or an appropriate buffer under aseptic conditions.[web:277][web:278][web:281]
  • Add solvent slowly down the vial wall and swirl gently; avoid vigorous shaking.

Reconstituted solutions

  • Store at 2–8 °C for short-term use, protected from light.[web:278][web:281]
  • For longer-term storage, aliquot and freeze at −20 °C or below to reduce freeze–thaw damage.[web:278][web:281]
  • Discard any solution that becomes cloudy, discoloured, or precipitates.

# GHRP-6 – Growth Hormone Releasing Peptide (Research Grade)

## Product overview

GHRP-6 is a synthetic hexapeptide used in laboratory research to study growth hormone secretagogue biology and ghrelin receptor signalling.[web:272][web:277][web:278] It acts as a GHSR-1a agonist and is commonly used in in-vitro systems and approved preclinical models to examine hormone release, appetite-related signalling, and tissue-protection pathways.[web:275][web:278][web:281]

This product is supplied strictly for research use only. It is not intended for human or veterinary use, dietary supplementation, or any therapeutic, cosmetic, or diagnostic purpose.[web:274][web:276][web:279]

## Identity and structure

GHRP-6 is a small peptide built from the sequence His-D-Trp-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys-NH2.[web:277][web:281] The D-amino acids and C-terminal amidation improve resistance to enzymatic breakdown and support its use in research models that need short-term but repeatable receptor stimulation.[web:272][web:281]

The peptide is widely described as a ghrelin-mimetic growth hormone secretagogue that binds the growth hormone secretagogue receptor type 1a (GHSR-1a).[web:275][web:278][web:284] Because it is less selective than some newer analogues, it is useful for studying broader ghrelin-receptor biology, including appetite and gastric effects in addition to GH release.[web:278][web:279][web:284]

## Scientific background (non-clinical)

### Growth hormone secretion

In research models, GHRP-6 stimulates pituitary somatotrophs and rapidly increases growth hormone release.[web:277][web:278] The signalling described in the literature includes Gq/11-linked pathways, phospholipase C activation, intracellular calcium mobilisation, and protein kinase C-related responses.[web:277][web:278][web:281]

GHRP-6 is also used in combination studies with GHRH analogues because dual activation can produce a stronger GH pulse than either signal alone.[web:274][web:278][web:284]

### Ghrelin receptor biology

GHRP-6 is valuable for examining ghrelin-receptor-mediated processes in the hypothalamus, pituitary, hippocampus, and peripheral tissues.[web:275][web:278][web:281] Research areas include appetite regulation, gastric motility, and broader neuroendocrine signalling linked to energy balance and motivation.[web:274][web:278][web:284]

The peptide’s shorter half-life and broad receptor activity make it especially useful in time-resolved studies where the onset and duration of secretagogue signalling matter.[web:278][web:281]

## Representative research applications

GHRP-6 is used as a laboratory tool to study ghrelin-receptor function and GH secretagogue pathways. Common preclinical research themes include:

- **GH release and pituitary signalling** – measuring somatotroph response, receptor activation, and second-messenger activity.[web:277][web:278]  
- **Appetite and energy-balance research** – examining hunger-related signalling and feeding behaviour in animal models.[web:275][web:278][web:279]  
- **Neuroprotection and memory studies** – investigating ghrelin-linked effects in learning, memory formation, and injury models.[web:274][web:275][web:277]  
- **Cardiac and tissue-protection models** – assessing broader cytoprotective, anti-fibrotic, and wound-repair pathways reported in preclinical literature.[web:271][web:272][web:273]

All uses remain restricted to in-vitro systems and approved preclinical studies.

## Handling, reconstitution, and storage

### Lyophilised powder

- Store unopened vials at −20 °C or below in a dry, dark environment.[web:277][web:278]  
- Keep containers tightly closed and protect from moisture.

### Reconstitution

- Reconstitute with sterile water, bacteriostatic water, or an appropriate buffer under aseptic conditions.[web:277][web:278][web:281]  
- Add solvent slowly along the vial wall and swirl gently until dissolved; avoid vigorous shaking.

### Reconstituted solutions

- Store at 2–8 °C for short-term use, protected from light.[web:278][web:281]  
- For longer-term use, aliquot and freeze at −20 °C or below to reduce freeze–thaw damage.[web:278][web:281]  
- Discard any solution showing turbidity, precipitation, or colour change.

Each laboratory should validate stability for its own assay conditions and follow internal SOPs.

What Is Kisspeptin-10?

A minimal, fully active peptide fragment built for focused receptor research.

The kisspeptin family includes several peptide fragments — kisspeptin-54, -14, -13, and -10. All of them are produced from the same KISS1 gene precursor, but they differ in length. KP-10 is the shortest of the group.

What makes it stand out is its C-terminal tail — the Arginine-Phenylalanine-amide (RFamide) sequence — which is the active part of the molecule shared across all kisspeptin fragments. This is what allows KP-10 to bind to the KISS1R (GPR54) receptor just as effectively as the longer versions, despite having fewer amino acids overall.

Because it's compact and fully characterized, KP-10 is a practical research tool. Researchers across reproductive biology, oncology, neuroscience, and cardiovascular science have used it as a clean, receptor-selective probe in both in vitro and in vivo settings.

How It Works at the Receptor

KP-10 binds selectively to KISS1R, a Gq-coupled G protein-coupled receptor. Once bound, it triggers a well-defined signaling cascade:

1
KISS1R Binding
High-affinity binding via the C-terminal RF-amide motif (IC₅₀ in the 1–10 nM range)
2
Gq Protein Activation
Phospholipase C is activated, generating IP₃ and DAG as second messengers
3
Calcium Mobilization
Rapid intracellular calcium release through IP₃-mediated pathways
4
Downstream Cascades
ERK1/2 MAPK, PKC, and PI3K/Akt pathways engaged depending on cell type
5
GnRH Neuron Activation
In the arcuate nucleus and AVPV, KISS1R stimulation drives GnRH pulse release

Structural Notes

The C-terminal Arg-Phe-NH₂ (RF-amide) tail is the essential binding pharmacophore. Without C-terminal amidation, receptor potency drops dramatically. The phenylalanine at position 6 and tryptophan at position 3 both contribute through aromatic stacking interactions with the receptor binding pocket. The sequence's mix of hydrophobic (Trp, Phe, Leu) and polar (Tyr, Asn, Ser) residues gives it an amphipathic character that supports both aqueous solubility and membrane-proximal receptor engagement.

Published Research Overview

A growing body of peer-reviewed work has documented KP-10's activity across multiple biological systems.

🧬 Reproductive Endocrinology

KP-10 is one of the most potent known stimulators of GnRH neuron activity. Studies in rodents, sheep, and humans confirm it drives a dose-dependent increase in LH, FSH, and testosterone through hypothalamic GnRH release. It's used to study puberty onset, menstrual cycle regulation, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, and the metabolic-reproductive interface — including how negative energy balance, leptin signaling, and obesity affect reproductive output.

🔬 Cancer & Metastasis Research

KISS1 expression is lower in metastatic forms of melanoma, gastric, pancreatic, bladder, ovarian, and prostate cancers — suggesting it plays a metastasis-suppressing role. KP-10 inhibits migration of both cancer cells and placental trophoblasts through modulation of GSK3β, FAK, and ERK1/2 pathways. In imaging research, DOTA-KP-10 analogues labeled with gallium-68 and lutetium-177 are being investigated as pan-tumor radiopharmaceuticals.

🧠 Neuroprotection

In vitro work shows KP-10 shields cholinergic neurons from alpha-synuclein-induced cell death and mitochondrial apoptosis — relevant to Lewy body dementia research. Some of these effects appear to be GPR54-independent, suggesting direct peptide-protein interactions. Separately, KP-10 has been shown to reduce cerebral aneurysm formation by inhibiting Egr-1, MMP-9, VEGF-A, and macrophage infiltration via the GPR54 receptor.

❤️ Cardiac Biology

KP-10 increases collagen content in heart muscle tissue by activating focal adhesion kinase (FAK) and inhibiting matrix metalloproteinases. This makes it a relevant tool in cardiac fibrosis and myocardial remodeling research.

⚡ Behavioral & Pharmacological Research

Animal pharmacology studies indicate KP-10 modulates impulsive behavior, compulsive actions, and reward-seeking through dopaminergic and serotonergic pathways. This is an emerging area with potential relevance to addiction science and compulsive behavior disorders.

Experimental Design Guidance

  • In vitro receptor binding: 1–100 nM is the typical working range
  • In vivo rodent ICV dosing: 1–100 nmol per administration
  • In vivo peripheral dosing: 0.1–10 nmol/kg
  • Always run dose-response curves before committing to a single dose
  • Include KISS1R antagonist arms to confirm receptor-mediated effects
  • Account for circadian timing — LH responses differ across the day
  • For sustained effects, use pulsed injections or continuous infusion
Storage & Reconstitution

Lyophilized Powder — Storage

  • Keep at -20°C to -80°C in the original sealed vial
  • Store away from light to prevent oxidation at Trp, Tyr, and Phe residues
  • Keep in a dry, desiccated environment — moisture degrades the powder
  • When stored correctly, lyophilized KP-10 is stable for 12+ months
  • Do not temperature-cycle an unopened vial

Reconstitution

  • Use sterile water, bacteriostatic water (0.9% benzyl alcohol), or PBS
  • Add solvent slowly down the inner wall of the vial — avoid pouring directly onto the cake
  • Swirl gently for 2–3 minutes until fully dissolved — no shaking
  • Target a final pH of 6.5–7.5 for optimal stability
  • Consider adding 0.1% BSA to working solutions to reduce adsorption losses in low-concentration experiments

Reconstituted Solution — Storage

  • Short-term: refrigerate at 4°C for up to 5–7 days (bacteriostatic water extends this window)
  • Long-term: freeze at -20°C in single-use aliquots
  • Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles — each cycle can reduce activity by 10–20%
  • Use aliquots from a fresh tube for each experiment where practical
  • Dilute solutions are more susceptible to degradation and should be prepared fresh when possible

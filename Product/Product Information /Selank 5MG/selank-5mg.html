What Is Selank?

An immunomodulatory-derived neuropeptide with a distinctive no-sedation anxiolytic profile in preclinical research.

Tuftsin is a short tetrapeptide — Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg — that the immune system produces by cleaving the Fc fragment of IgG antibodies. It was first characterised for its role in activating phagocytic immune cells, particularly macrophages and neutrophils. Selank extends this sequence with a C-terminal Pro-Gly-Pro tail, adding the structural protection needed to survive in plasma long enough to produce measurable biological effects. The result is a molecule that carries tuftsin's immunomodulatory capability into a broader pharmacological space that includes neurological research.

Three of Selank's seven amino acids are proline, which gives the peptide an unusually rigid backbone for its size and slows its degradation by common peptidases. The plasma half-life of approximately 20–30 minutes is short by drug standards but represents a meaningful extension over tuftsin, which is cleared within minutes.

In preclinical anxiety models, Selank produces anxiolytic-like outcomes without motor impairment, sedation, or the tolerance patterns seen with benzodiazepines. This makes it pharmacologically interesting as a probe for studying non-GABA-A agonist anxiolytic mechanisms. Beyond anxiety research, it is used in studies of stress physiology, cognitive enhancement, BDNF-mediated neuroplasticity, immune-brain axis communication, and cytokine gene modulation.

Mechanism of Action

Selank engages multiple neurotransmitter systems simultaneously — a multi-pathway profile that distinguishes it from single-target anxiolytics.

GABAergic Modulation Without Direct Agonism

Benzodiazepines produce anxiolysis by binding allosteric sites on GABA-A receptors, amplifying chloride channel conductance — which also causes sedation, motor impairment, memory disruption, and tolerance. Selank produces functionally similar anxiolytic-like outcomes in preclinical models without these associated effects. It appears to modulate GABAergic tone indirectly — through effects on GABAergic interneuron activity, GABAergic receptor expression changes, and the opioid-GABAergic interaction — rather than direct receptor binding. This mechanistic separation is precisely what makes it useful for studying alternative anxiolytic pathways.

Enkephalinase Inhibition and Augmented Opioid Tone

Selank inhibits enkephalinase (neutral endopeptidase), slowing the synaptic degradation of endogenous enkephalins. Enkephalins are short endogenous opioid peptides that act on mu and delta receptors in limbic and midbrain circuits regulating stress, mood, and pain. By slowing their breakdown, Selank raises their local concentration and prolongs their receptor engagement — augmenting tonic opioid signalling without introducing exogenous opioids. This enkephalinase inhibition is considered one of the primary drivers of Selank's anxiolytic and stress-protective effects.

Serotonin System Restoration

In chronic stress models, Selank restores depleted serotonin and serotonin metabolite levels in midbrain structures. The mechanism is likely indirect, operating through enkephalin-serotonin circuit interactions in limbic regions. This restoration of serotonergic tone correlates with normalised anxiety behaviour and is a key neurochemical endpoint in Selank stress research.

BDNF Upregulation — The Cognitive Correlate

Selank increases BDNF protein in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex. BDNF activates TrkB receptors and drives synaptic strengthening, long-term potentiation, and neuronal survival signalling. In Kolik et al.'s (2019) ethanol-impairment study, Selank treatment maintained hippocampal and prefrontal BDNF levels in ethanol-exposed rats, and this correlated directly with protection of spatial memory performance. This neurotrophic mechanism provides the molecular underpinning for Selank's observed cognitive effects in challenge models.

Cytokine Gene Modulation — The Immune Connection

Kolomin et al. (2023) demonstrated that Selank and its fragments produce widespread changes in chemokine, cytokine, and cytokine receptor gene expression — suggesting the peptide operates at the transcriptional level in immune regulation, not just as a cytokine blocker. In stress models, this translates to normalisation of the IL-6/IL-10 ratio, restoration of phagocytic neutrophil activity, and protection of immune organ integrity. The tuftsin core's classical macrophage-stimulating activity is likely preserved and integrated into this broader immunomodulatory profile.

Published Research Overview

Select findings from preclinical literature on Selank's neurological and immunological effects.

Anxiety Models
Anxiolytic-Like Effects Without Sedation (Elevated Plus-Maze, Open-Field)
At 100–300 µg/kg (i.p.), Selank consistently reduces time in closed arms of the elevated plus-maze and increases open-field exploration in rodents — without reducing locomotor activity, causing ataxia, or producing sedation. This sedation-free profile is maintained even at higher doses where benzodiazepines would produce clear motor impairment, making Selank useful for studying anxiolytic circuits without confounding sedation variables.
Cognitive Protection
Ethanol-Induced Memory Impairment and BDNF (Kolik et al., 2019)
Selank protected spatial memory performance in rats with ethanol-induced cognitive impairment. The protection correlated directly with maintained BDNF protein levels in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex in treated animals, while vehicle-treated ethanol-exposed animals showed both BDNF depletion and memory deficits. This is one of the clearest demonstrations of Selank's BDNF-cognitive connection in the published literature.
Stress Physiology
Organ Protection Under Chronic Restraint Stress (Mukhina et al., 2020)
Chronic restraint stress in rats produces measurable morphological changes in the colon. Selank treatment reduced these structural changes, indicating systemic stress-protective effects beyond the CNS. Separately, Selank has been shown to reduce adrenal hypertrophy, lower corticosterone, and restore neutrophil phagocytic activity in acute traumatic stress models.
Immunomodulation
Chemokine and Cytokine Gene Expression (Kolomin et al., 2023)
Selank and its metabolic fragments produce significant changes in the expression of genes encoding chemokines, cytokines, and their receptors — suggesting transcription-level immunoregulatory activity. This work expands Selank's known immunological footprint beyond simple cytokine level changes to include gene regulatory networks involved in immune cell trafficking and inflammatory signalling.

Storage & Reconstitution

Lyophilized Powder

  • Store at –20°C to –80°C in the original sealed vial
  • Protect from light and moisture; desiccated environment required
  • Stable for 12+ months at –20°C under proper conditions
  • Avoid temperature cycling of sealed vials

Reconstitution

  • Use sterile water, bacteriostatic water (0.9% benzyl alcohol), or PBS
  • Target final pH 6.0–7.5 for optimal stability
  • Add solvent slowly down the inner vial wall; swirl gently for 1–2 minutes
  • Do not shake or vortex; solution should appear clear and colourless
  • Wipe the vial stopper with 70% isopropyl alcohol before each use

After Reconstitution

  • Store at 4°C for up to 7 days; prepare single-use aliquots for freeze storage
  • Freeze at –20°C in aliquots for longer storage; maximum 2–3 freeze-thaw cycles
  • Discard if solution becomes cloudy, develops particulates, or changes colour
  • Minimise time outside the refrigerator during active use

Administration Route Consideration

  • Intranasal administration shows higher CNS bioavailability than parenteral routes in some rodent models — consider route selection based on whether central or peripheral effects are the primary research question
  • Intraperitoneal is the most common route in published rodent anxiety and cognitive studies

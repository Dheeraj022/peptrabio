## What Is Selank?

Selank is a synthetic seven-amino acid peptide derived from tuftsin — a naturally occurring tetrapeptide (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg) that is enzymatically cleaved from the Fc fragment of immunoglobulin G and serves as an endogenous regulator of phagocytic immune cell activity. Rather than simply reproducing the tuftsin sequence, Selank extends it with a C-terminal Pro-Gly-Pro tail designed specifically to improve metabolic stability. The result is a structurally stiffened, protease-resistant molecule that retains tuftsin's immunomodulatory core while exhibiting a substantially broader range of biological activity in the central nervous system.

The full sequence — Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro — contains three proline residues that impose structural rigidity and resistance to enzymatic degradation. This design gives Selank a plasma half-life of approximately 20–30 minutes, compared to the few minutes typical of unmodified tuftsin. The peptide was developed at Russia's Institute of Molecular Genetics, where it was initially studied as a potential anxiolytic research compound with a pharmacological profile distinct from classical benzodiazepines.

What makes Selank scientifically interesting as a research tool is the combination of effects it produces in preclinical models: anxiolytic-like behaviour without sedation, cognitive enhancement without stimulant properties, immunomodulatory activity traceable to its tuftsin origin, and BDNF upregulation in hippocampal and cortical regions. These effects appear to be mediated through GABAergic modulation, enkephalinase inhibition, serotonin system interactions, and neurotrophic factor regulation — a multi-pathway profile that is difficult to replicate with more targeted single-mechanism compounds.

Selank is supplied as a white to off-white lyophilized powder in a sealed 5mg research vial. It is for laboratory use only.

## Structural Features

### Three Prolines and Why They Matter

Proline's cyclic ring structure restricts conformational flexibility in peptide chains, making adjacent peptide bonds less accessible to proteases. Having three proline residues in a seven-residue peptide gives Selank a degree of structural rigidity unusual for such a small molecule. Positions 3, 5, and 7 — all proline — protect the bonds between them from enzymatic cleavage. This is the primary structural reason Selank outlasts tuftsin by many minutes to tens of minutes in plasma.

### The Pro-Gly-Pro Extension

The addition of Pro-Gly-Pro to the C-terminus of the tuftsin core was a deliberate stability engineering decision. Glycine between the two prolines allows some local conformational flexibility while the flanking prolines block carboxypeptidase access. Beyond stability, this tail appears to contribute to the peptide's CNS activity: some metabolic fragments of Selank retain biological activity, suggesting the extension is not simply inert scaffolding but actively participates in receptor or target interactions.

### Positive Charge and Surface Interactions

Lysine at position 2 and arginine at position 4 give Selank a net charge of +2 at physiological pH. This positive character facilitates interaction with negatively charged components on cell membranes and extracellular matrix — which may contribute to cellular uptake and membrane proximity during binding events. The basic residues also contribute to aqueous solubility, though working at low concentrations still requires attention to adsorption on standard plastic surfaces.

## Mechanism of Action

### GABAergic Modulation Without Direct Receptor Agonism

Selank's most pharmacologically distinctive property is its ability to produce anxiolytic-like outcomes through the GABAergic system without acting as a direct GABA-A receptor agonist. Benzodiazepines achieve anxiolysis by binding allosteric sites on GABA-A receptors and amplifying chloride conductance — which also produces sedation, motor impairment, and tolerance with chronic use. Selank does not show these side effects in preclinical models. It appears to modulate GABAergic transmission indirectly — through effects on interneuron activity and receptor expression changes — producing functionally similar anxiolytic outcomes through a mechanistically distinct route. This makes it valuable for studying alternative anxiety circuit interventions.

### Enkephalinase Inhibition and Opioid Tone

Selank inhibits enkephalinase (neutral endopeptidase), the enzyme that degrades endogenous enkephalins in the synapse. By slowing enkephalin breakdown, Selank effectively raises tonic opioid signalling through mu and delta opioid receptors without introducing exogenous opioids. Enkephalins modulate mood, stress responses, and pain perception in limbic and midbrain circuits, and augmented enkephalin signalling is thought to contribute substantially to Selank's anxiolytic and stress-protective preclinical profile.

### Serotonin System Restoration

In chronic stress and immobilisation models, Selank restores serotonin and serotonin metabolite levels in midbrain structures where these are depleted by prolonged stress exposure. The mechanism is likely indirect — via the enkephalin-serotonin interaction in limbic circuits — rather than direct serotonin transporter or receptor binding. This restoration of serotonergic tone in stress-affected regions correlates with normalised anxiety behaviour in animal models, making it a relevant endpoint for researchers studying the neurochemical basis of stress resilience.

### BDNF Upregulation

Selank increases BDNF protein levels in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex in preclinical studies. BDNF is the primary TrkB receptor ligand and a central mediator of synaptic plasticity, long-term potentiation, and neuronal survival. Kolik et al. (2019) demonstrated that Selank's protection against ethanol-induced memory impairment directly correlated with preserved hippocampal and prefrontal BDNF levels — providing a clear neurotrophic mechanism for the observed cognitive effects in learning and memory models.

### Immunomodulatory Activity

Reflecting its tuftsin origin, Selank modulates cytokine balance in stress conditions. It normalises the IL-6/IL-10 ratio in acute stress models, reducing the pro-inflammatory IL-6 elevation while supporting anti-inflammatory IL-10 responses. Kolomin et al. (2023) identified broad changes in chemokine and cytokine receptor gene expression under Selank treatment, suggesting it operates at the transcriptional level in immune gene regulation rather than simply blocking individual cytokine pathways. This immune-brain axis activity is a key part of what distinguishes Selank from purely neurological research tools.

## Research Applications

### 1. Anxiolytic Mechanism Research

In the elevated plus-maze, open-field test, and social interaction test, Selank at 100–300 µg/kg (intraperitoneal) consistently reduces anxiety-like behaviour without decreasing locomotor activity or producing sedation. This clean anxiolytic profile allows researchers to study non-benzodiazepine anxiolytic mechanisms in controlled conditions. Comparative designs pairing Selank with diazepam, GABA-A receptor antagonists, or opioid receptor blockers allow pathway-level attribution of the anxiolytic effect to specific neurotransmitter systems.

### 2. Stress Physiology Research

Selank reduces multiple physiological indicators of chronic stress exposure in animal models: it attenuates adrenal hypertrophy, reduces corticosterone elevation, restores normal monoamine levels in stress-affected brain regions, and reverses stress-induced morphological changes in peripheral organs including the colon and liver. Mukhina et al. (2020) documented specific protection of large intestinal tissue structure under chronic restraint stress. These multi-system effects make Selank useful for studying how peptide modulators coordinate CNS, endocrine, immune, and peripheral organ responses to sustained stress.

### 3. Cognitive Research — Ethanol Impairment and Stress Models

In learning and memory paradigms, Selank improves both acquisition and retention in passive avoidance and Morris water maze tests. The most mechanistically informative work involves challenge models — particularly ethanol-induced cognitive impairment — where the correlation between Selank treatment, maintained BDNF levels, and improved performance provides a testable neurotrophin hypothesis for the observed cognitive protection. Dose-response relationships in these models have been characterised at 100–300 µg/kg.

### 4. Immune System and Neuroimmune Research

Selank modulates T-cell and B-cell activity, phagocytic neutrophil function, and cytokine gene expression in stress models. It restores cellular and humoral immune responses suppressed by acute traumatic stress, positioning it as a research tool for studying stress-induced immunosuppression mechanisms. The tuftsin connection gives it particular relevance for macrophage and neutrophil function studies, where the tuftsin core's classical immunostimulatory role can be compared against Selank's modified, extended activity profile.

### 5. Neuroprotection and Neuroinflammation

In glial cell cultures, Selank modulates cytokine release and promotes neuroprotective signalling. Combined with its BDNF upregulation in hippocampal tissue, this creates a multi-pathway neuroprotective profile relevant to oxidative stress, excitotoxicity, and stress-induced neuronal damage models. The peptide's non-sedating profile makes it practically useful in behavioural neuroprotection paradigms where sedative compounds would confound test outcomes.

## Intranasal vs. Systemic Administration

A practical note for experimental design: intranasal administration of Selank demonstrates higher CNS bioavailability in some rodent models compared to parenteral routes. This is relevant because the olfactory epithelium provides a direct route from the nasal mucosa to the olfactory bulb and adjacent brain structures, bypassing the blood-brain barrier limitations that affect systemically administered peptides. Researchers investigating central mechanisms may benefit from comparing intranasal versus subcutaneous or intraperitoneal routes to assess whether peripheral or central receptor engagement drives the observed effects.

## Comparing Selank and Semax

Both Selank and Semax are synthetic heptapeptides developed by the same Russian research institution. They have distinct parent sequences — Selank derives from tuftsin (immune-origin), while Semax derives from ACTH(4-10) (pituitary-origin) — but overlap in some research applications, particularly BDNF upregulation and stress response modulation. Key differences:

| Feature | Selank | Semax |
|---|---|---|
| Parent compound | Tuftsin (IgG Fc fragment) | ACTH(4-10) (pituitary hormone) |
| Receptor pathway | GABAergic, enkephalinase, serotonin | Melanocortin MC₄R/MC₅R |
| Primary profile | Anxiolytic, stress-protective, immunomodulatory | Neurotrophic, cognitive, neuroprotective |
| BDNF effect | Yes — hippocampus and PFC | Yes — hippocampus and basal forebrain |
| Immune activity | Yes — tuftsin-derived | Moderate — via neuroinflammation |
| Sedation risk | No | No |

## Storage and Reconstitution

### Lyophilized Powder

Store sealed vials at –20°C to –80°C. Protect from light and moisture; a desiccated environment is required. Stable for 12+ months at –20°C when stored correctly. Avoid temperature cycling of sealed vials.

### Reconstitution

Use sterile water, bacteriostatic water, or PBS. Target pH 6.0–7.5. Add solvent slowly down the inner vial wall; swirl gently for 1–2 minutes. Do not shake or vortex. Wipe the vial stopper with 70% isopropyl alcohol before each use. Solution should be clear and colourless.

### After Reconstitution

Store at 4°C for up to 7 days. Freeze at –20°C in single-use aliquots for longer storage. Maximum 2–3 freeze-thaw cycles. Discard any solution that becomes cloudy, develops visible particulates, or changes colour.

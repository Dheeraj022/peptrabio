What Is Semax?

A compact, chemically stabilised fragment of ACTH engineered to carry neurotrophic and neuroprotective activity without hormonal side effects.

Adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) is a 39-residue pituitary hormone best known for driving cortisol production. But within its sequence, the seven-residue stretch at positions 4–10 — Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro — turns out to carry the neurological activity of the full hormone. Research in the 1970s and 1980s established that this fragment engages melanocortin receptors in the brain and influences memory, attention, and stress adaptation in animal models, without triggering the adrenocortical activity that makes full-length ACTH unsuitable for CNS research. Semax is this fragment, stabilised by N-terminal acetylation.

The acetyl group at the N-terminus blocks aminopeptidase-mediated degradation, extending the working lifespan of the peptide in plasma and tissue. Two proline residues within the sequence add structural rigidity. Together, these features make Semax a practical laboratory research tool where the native ACTH(4-10) fragment would be too short-lived to study.

The five research areas where Semax has the most published preclinical evidence are: upregulation of BDNF and NGF in neural tissue, modulation of dopaminergic and serotonergic systems, neuroprotection in cerebral ischaemia models, stress and immune physiology, and inhibition of amyloid-beta aggregation in Alzheimer's disease contexts.

Mechanism of Action

How a seven-amino acid peptide produces broad neurological effects through melanocortin receptors and downstream signalling cascades.

Melanocortin Receptor Binding

The core ACTH(4-10) sequence in Semax — particularly the phenylalanine and histidine residues — engages melanocortin receptors MC₄R and MC₅R. These are Gs-protein-coupled receptors distributed across the hippocampus, hypothalamus, frontal cortex, and brainstem. Receptor activation raises intracellular cAMP, which activates protein kinase A (PKA) and drives CREB phosphorylation. The resulting transcriptional changes — including upregulation of BDNF and NGF — are understood to be the primary driver of Semax's cognitive and neuroprotective effects in preclinical models.

BDNF Upregulation and TrkB Signalling

BDNF is the principal ligand for TrkB (tropomyosin receptor kinase B), a receptor tyrosine kinase expressed on neurons throughout the brain. When Semax raises BDNF levels in hippocampal and cortical tissue, it indirectly amplifies TrkB signalling, which promotes long-term potentiation, synapse formation, and anti-apoptotic cell survival programmes. Dolotov et al. (2006) confirmed specific binding of Semax to basal forebrain tissue with direct measurement of BDNF protein elevation — establishing this as a genuine receptor-mediated effect rather than non-specific peptide activity.

Dopamine and Serotonin System Effects

Semax increases the turnover rates of dopamine and serotonin in the striatum and frontal cortex — measurable by elevated metabolite-to-neurotransmitter ratios and enzyme activity. It induces tyrosine hydroxylase, the rate-limiting enzyme in catecholamine synthesis, and alters dopamine D1 and D2 receptor density in specific brain regions. These monoaminergic changes contribute to the normalisation of anxiety-like behaviour and stress response dysregulation observed in chronic stress models, where baseline dopamine and serotonin levels are depressed.

Anti-Inflammatory Pathway Coordination

In both glial cell cultures and in vivo ischaemia models, Semax reduces the production of IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-1β while promoting anti-inflammatory mediator balance. Genome-wide transcriptional analysis in focal cerebral ischaemia (Medvedeva et al., 2014) showed that Semax upregulates immune coordination genes, chemokine signalling networks, and vascular remodelling programmes — suggesting the peptide does not simply dampen inflammation but helps organise it toward a reparative pattern rather than a destructive one. This mechanistic nuance is important when interpreting Semax's anti-inflammatory effects in experimental contexts.

Published Research Overview

Key research areas and select study findings from the preclinical literature.

Cerebral Ischaemia
Dose-Dependent Neuroprotection in Focal Stroke Models
At 50–250 µg/kg (intraperitoneal), Semax reduced infarct volume by 30–50% in rat focal cerebral ischaemia models, improved neurological recovery scores, and elevated BDNF and NGF in peri-infarct regions. Inflammatory infiltration was reduced and genome-wide transcriptomics confirmed upregulation of vascular, immune coordination, and chemokine genes consistent with active tissue repair.
Cognitive Models
Learning and Memory Enhancement (Morris Maze, Passive Avoidance)
At 100–300 µg/kg, Semax improved acquisition speed and memory retention in both normal and cognitively impaired rats. Effects were strongest in ischaemia and neurotoxin impairment models and correlated directly with BDNF protein levels in hippocampal tissue. Kirchev (2023) documented enhanced cognitive recovery in chronic brain ischaemia using Semax in combination with hopantenic acid.
Alzheimer's Disease Models
Amyloid-Beta Aggregation Inhibition and Cholinergic Neuron Support
Semax binds Cu²⁺ ions and inhibits Aβ:Cu²⁺ complex formation, a step involved in amyloid oligomer nucleation. In vitro studies showed reduced fibrillogenesis in membrane models (Sciacca et al., 2022). Separately, basal forebrain cholinergic neuron survival was increased 1.5–1.7 fold, and choline acetyltransferase activity was stimulated — findings relevant to the selective neuronal vulnerability in Alzheimer's dementia.
Stress & Immunity
Stress-Protective and Immunomodulatory Effects in Social Stress Models
Semax normalised elevated cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α), reduced adrenal hypertrophy and gastric ulceration in chronic stress models, and decreased lipid peroxidation in immune organs (thymus, spleen). It restored monoamine levels in stress-affected brain regions and selectively blocked the opioid form of stress-induced analgesia without altering the behavioural stress response — indicating mechanistic specificity in its stress modulation.

Storage & Reconstitution
🔬
Met¹ Oxidation — Key Handling PriorityThe methionine at position 1 is Semax's primary chemical vulnerability. Oxidation to methionine sulfoxide alters receptor affinity. Use deaerated solvents, minimise air headspace in reconstituted vials, and work promptly after thawing.

Lyophilized Powder

  • Store at –20°C to –80°C in the original sealed vial
  • Protect from light, moisture, and oxygen — inert gas (nitrogen or argon) atmosphere recommended for long-term storage
  • Stable for 12+ months at –20°C under proper conditions
  • Avoid temperature cycling of sealed vials

Reconstitution

  • Use sterile water, bacteriostatic water, or PBS; target pH 6.0–7.0
  • Use deaerated solvent where possible to minimise dissolved oxygen exposure at Met¹
  • Add solvent slowly down the inner wall of the vial; swirl gently 1–2 minutes
  • Do not shake or vortex; solution should be clear and colourless when ready

After Reconstitution

  • Store at 4°C for up to 14 days in sealed single-use aliquots
  • Freeze at –20°C for longer storage; maximum 3–4 freeze-thaw cycles
  • Low-concentration ascorbic acid may be added to protect Met¹ in extended incubation experiments
  • Discard if solution appears cloudy, discoloured, or contains particulates
  • Follow standard PPE and biosafety protocols throughout handling

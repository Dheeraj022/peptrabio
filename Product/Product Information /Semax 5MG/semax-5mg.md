## What Is Semax?

Semax is a synthetic heptapeptide built from the core of adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), specifically residues 4 through 10 of ACTH's sequence — Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro — with an N-terminal acetyl group added to slow enzymatic breakdown. Despite being only seven amino acids long, the peptide carries a surprising range of biological activity in preclinical models, particularly in the areas of neurotrophic factor expression, neurotransmitter modulation, neuroprotection, and stress physiology.

Semax was originally developed at Russia's Institute of Molecular Genetics during the 1980s and 1990s as part of an effort to isolate the brain-relevant activity of ACTH without the hormonal side effects — particularly the cortisol-releasing action — that accompany the full 39-amino acid peptide. By working from the ACTH(4-10) fragment, researchers preserved the melanocortin receptor-engaging properties relevant to cognition and neuroprotection while eliminating the endocrine activity that makes full-length ACTH impractical as a central nervous system research tool.

The N-terminal acetylation that defines Semax as a modified compound (rather than simply the raw ACTH 4–10 fragment) serves two purposes: it blocks aminopeptidase-mediated degradation at the free N-terminus, and it adds modest lipophilicity that improves the peptide's ability to cross the blood-brain barrier when delivered intranasally. The two proline residues at positions 5 and 7 contribute structural rigidity that further resists protease attack.

In laboratory settings, Semax is studied for its effects on BDNF and NGF expression in neural tissue, its modulation of dopamine and serotonin systems, its protective effects in ischaemia and excitotoxicity models, its anti-inflammatory activity in neuroinflammation paradigms, and its interactions with amyloid-beta aggregation pathways relevant to Alzheimer's disease research.

## Structural Design

### The ACTH Fragment Connection

Adrenocorticotropic hormone is a 39-amino acid pituitary peptide primarily known for driving cortisol release from the adrenal glands. But research in the 1970s and 1980s established that the segment spanning positions 4 through 10 — Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro — retains the ability to engage brain melanocortin receptors and influence memory, attention, and stress adaptation in animal models, without triggering adrenocortical activity. This established the ACTH(4-10) fragment as a scientifically interesting minimal sequence for neurological research. Semax is a stabilised version of this fragment.

### N-Terminal Acetylation

The acetyl group capping the N-terminus of the methionine residue is not decorative — it blocks the sequential removal of amino acids from the N-terminal end by aminopeptidases. These enzymes are ubiquitous in plasma, tissue, and cerebrospinal fluid and would otherwise rapidly degrade the free ACTH(4-10) fragment. Acetylation is what makes Semax a viable research tool for in vivo studies; without it, the peptide would be cleared before producing measurable neurological effects. The modification also increases lipophilicity slightly, supporting blood-brain barrier transit.

### Two Prolines and Conformational Stability

Proline's cyclic structure prevents rotation around its backbone nitrogen bond, imposing rigidity on the surrounding peptide chain. Having two prolines in a seven-residue peptide — at positions 5 and 7 — gives Semax an unusually constrained conformation for its size. This structural rigidity contributes to the molecule's resistance to peptidase cleavage at these positions, complementing the N-terminal acetylation.

### The Methionine Oxidation Risk

Met¹ at the N-terminus is Semax's principal chemical vulnerability. Under aerobic conditions, the thioether group of methionine oxidises to methionine sulfoxide, which alters the steric properties of the N-terminal region and can reduce receptor affinity and biological potency. This makes anaerobic handling of working solutions important, particularly for in vitro experiments where oxidative conditions in incubation buffers or cell culture media might otherwise degrade the peptide over time.

## Mechanism of Action

### Melanocortin Receptor Activation

Semax engages melanocortin receptors MC₄R and MC₅R, which are Gs-protein-coupled receptors driving adenylate cyclase and raising intracellular cyclic AMP (cAMP). MC₄R is expressed in the hypothalamus, hippocampus, brainstem, and cortex, where its activation has been linked to improved attention, motivated behaviour, and learning. Elevated cAMP downstream of MC₄R activation drives PKA-dependent transcriptional programmes — including the upregulation of BDNF and NGF — that are thought to underlie the sustained neurological effects seen in preclinical studies.

### BDNF and NGF Upregulation

Semax's most consistently documented effect in rodent brain tissue is an increase in BDNF protein levels in the hippocampus, frontal cortex, and basal forebrain. BDNF is the primary ligand for the TrkB receptor and drives synaptic strengthening, long-term potentiation, and neuronal survival. Dolotov et al. (2006) demonstrated specific binding of Semax to basal forebrain tissue with direct upregulation of BDNF protein — providing mechanistic grounding for the cognitive effects observed in behavioural studies. NGF upregulation has been reported in cortical preparations as well, particularly following ischaemic challenges.

### Monoaminergic System Effects

Semax increases dopamine and serotonin turnover in the striatum and frontal cortex, induces tyrosine hydroxylase expression (the rate-limiting step in catecholamine biosynthesis), and alters dopamine receptor density in specific brain regions. These changes in monoaminergic tone contribute to the stress-normalising effects seen in rodent models — restored dopamine and serotonin levels in stress-affected regions correlate with reduced anxiety-like behaviour in standard laboratory measures.

### Anti-Inflammatory and Neuroprotective Actions

In glial cell cultures and in vivo neuroinflammation models, Semax reduces IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-1β production and promotes anti-inflammatory mediator balance. In neuronal cultures, it protects against glutamate-induced excitotoxic cell death, preserves mitochondrial membrane potential, and reduces caspase-3 activation. Genome-wide transcriptional analysis following focal cerebral ischaemia in rats showed Semax upregulates immune coordination genes, chemokine signalling, and vascular remodelling pathways — suggesting it helps organise a reparative rather than simply anti-inflammatory response in injured tissue.

## Research Applications

### 1. Cerebral Ischaemia and Stroke Models

In focal cerebral ischaemia rat models, Semax at 50–250 µg/kg (intraperitoneal) reduced infarct volume by 30–50% in a dose-dependent manner and improved neurological recovery scores. BDNF and NGF levels were elevated in peri-infarct regions, and inflammatory infiltration was reduced. These findings position Semax as a tool for studying the neurotrophic factor-mediated component of neuroprotection following ischaemic injury, and for comparing intervention strategies that target BDNF signalling versus other protective pathways.

### 2. Learning, Memory, and Cognitive Models

In Morris water maze and passive avoidance paradigms, Semax improved acquisition speed and memory retention in both normal rats and models with ischaemia- or neurotoxin-induced impairment. Doses of 100–300 µg/kg were effective across age groups. The improvement in cognitive performance correlated with BDNF protein levels in hippocampal tissue, supporting the hypothesis that neurotrophic factor induction is a primary driver of Semax's cognitive effects. This makes it useful for controlled comparisons of cognitive interventions targeting BDNF expression.

### 3. Stress Physiology and Immune-Stress Research

In chronic social and informational stress models, Semax normalised anxiety behaviour in elevated plus-maze and open-field tests, restored monoamine levels in affected brain regions, and reduced cytokine elevations (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α) in immune tissues. It also reduced adrenal hypertrophy and gastric ulceration markers in stressed animals, and decreased lipid peroxidation in the thymus and spleen — indicators of oxidative immune organ damage under stress. Semax selectively blocked the opioid form of stress-induced analgesia without altering the behavioural stress response, showing mechanistic specificity in its stress modulation activity.

### 4. Alzheimer's Disease and Neurodegeneration Research

Semax binds Cu²⁺ ions and in doing so interferes with the formation of Aβ:Cu²⁺ complexes — a step implicated in the nucleation of amyloid oligomers and fibrils in Alzheimer's pathology. Sciacca et al. (2022) demonstrated inhibition of fibril formation and reduction of toxic oligomeric species in membrane model systems. Separately, Semax has been shown to increase the survival of cholinergic neurons in basal forebrain cultures by approximately 1.5–1.7 fold and to stimulate choline acetyltransferase activity — findings relevant to the selective vulnerability of this neuronal population in Alzheimer's dementia.

### 5. Neuroinflammation Research

Semax reduces pro-inflammatory cytokine production in LPS-stimulated microglia and astrocytes, protects against excitotoxic and oxidative neuronal damage in culture, and alters gene expression in ischaemic brain tissue in ways consistent with coordinated immune and vascular repair rather than blanket suppression. The genome-wide transcriptomic dataset from Medvedeva et al. (2014) provides a valuable starting reference for researchers designing gene expression studies with Semax in ischaemic or inflammatory models.

## Comparing Semax and NA-Semax Amidate

Standard Semax has an N-terminal acetyl group but a free C-terminal carboxylic acid. The amidate variant — N-Acetyl Semax Amidate — adds C-terminal amidation, which blocks carboxypeptidase attack from the C-terminal end. This bilateral protection significantly extends plasma stability (approximately 8–12 times more stable than standard Semax) and biological activity duration (8–16 hours versus 15–20 minutes for unmodified forms). The trade-off is that the amidate variant is a more modified molecule, which complicates mechanistic attribution. Researchers who want to study Semax's effects over acute timeframes, or who want to compare modified versus less-modified compounds, should consider the standard Semax format. For studies requiring sustained effects with simplified dosing, the amidate variant may be more practical.

## Storage and Reconstitution

### Lyophilized Powder

Store sealed vials at –20°C to –80°C. Protect from light, moisture, and air — inert gas atmosphere (nitrogen or argon) is recommended for long-term storage. Stable for 12+ months at –20°C. Avoid temperature cycling of sealed vials.

### Reconstitution

Use sterile water, bacteriostatic water, or PBS. Use deaerated solvent where possible to reduce dissolved oxygen that could oxidise Met¹. Target pH 6.0–7.0 for optimal chemical stability. Add solvent slowly down the inner wall of the vial; swirl gently for 1–2 minutes until fully dissolved. Do not shake or vortex. Solution should appear clear and colourless.

### After Reconstitution

Store at 4°C for up to 14 days. For longer storage, freeze in single-use aliquots at –20°C; limit freeze-thaw cycles to 3–4 maximum. Antioxidants (e.g., low-concentration ascorbic acid) may be added to working solutions for extended incubation experiments where methionine oxidation is a concern. Discard any solution that appears cloudy, discoloured, or shows particulates.
